1. Scenario and exposure assessment summary

This report compares two climate scenarios for 2035–2044 and 2050–2059, examining temperature differences, exceedance frequency and sensitivity to calibration and model choice. Calculations are completed within each model before summarizing model distributions, providing the temperature basis for population and land-exposure screening.

The near window is 2035–2044 and the far window is 2050–2059. Both assessments use climate-model scenarios without extrapolating a short-term time-series trend. The main ensemble contains 14 models. CMCC-CM2-SR5 is available only as unrevised v1.0 data and has a version-related temperature-offset anomaly. Its influence is tested separately in a sensitivity analysis including all 15 models.

This report combines cell-level temperature with population, built surface and cropland to identify locations for priority checks. Contributions and list stability are calculated separately for the three exposure layers, while a common holdout evaluates correction error. The resulting lists guide the collection of resident, asset and agricultural data.

2. Uses of scenario analysis

Temperature differences compare pathways; absolute exceedance frequency informs conditional follow-up checks. Pairing and counting are performed within each model.

3. Windows and ensembles

Windows are 2035–2044 and 2050–2059 under ssp126 and ssp585. The main ensemble has 14 models; all 15 including CMCC form a sensitivity. Production uses February 2000–December 2024, while independent validation trains on 2000–2013 and tests on 2014–2024.

4. Exceedance method and denominators

Observations are MODIS daytime land surface temperature (LST), February 2000–December 2024; model input is NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 near-surface air temperature (tas). A clear-sky daytime retrieval at an approximately 1 km central pixel differs from the area and daily mean represented by a 0.25° tas cell. Monthly offsets align model means to the LST reference without removing all variable and sampling differences. Product definitions: MODIS and NEX-GDDP-CMIP6.

Spatial totals use the union of the full extracted cells. Population and built surface use 2020 data, and cropland uses 2021 land cover. These population and land distributions stay fixed in future scenarios while temperature changes. GHS and GPW allocate population differently, so similar population-weighted frequencies can accompany different exposure totals.

Population and land area are summed over the full study cells. Population is rounded only for display; calculations retain the original decimals. Cropland area is the cropland fraction multiplied by each cell’s spherical area.

Each cell uses the 90th percentile (P90) of all its valid monthly temperatures as its threshold. Both observations and future scenarios count only months strictly above that threshold. Exceedance probabilities are estimated separately for each calendar month and then summed across the twelve months. The result, cᵢ, is the cell's annual mean number of exceedance months, measured in months/year.

Let wᵢ be the population or relevant land area in cell i. Both indicators below are calculated within each model before summarizing their distribution across models.

Weighted annual exceedance frequency describes the average frequency at the locations occupied by people or land. Its unit is months/year:

∑iwici∑iwi

Total exceedance exposure includes both frequency and exposure size. Population totals use people·months/year, and land totals use m²·months/year:

∑iwici

The ratio of population-weighted to area-weighted frequency, and its reciprocal, are calculated within each model before taking the model median. Dividing the two frequency medians in a table would give a different statistic.

One cell–calendar-month combination in each of TK and AU has no historical observation. Both observations and models exclude these combinations on the same reference support, while the spatial aggregation remains fixed. Full-year missing-data bounds are assessed separately. The metric describes monthly mean LST exceedances of a local historical threshold, not days in a heatwave.

Weighted annual exceedance frequency describes the average frequency at population or land locations. Total exceedance exposure additionally includes population size or land area. Section 4 gives the formulas and units. Both the population-to-area frequency ratio and its reciprocal must be calculated within each model before summarizing the model median.

6. Calibration sensitivity and time resampling

Calibration windows change some directions. AU near-window population “future minus observed” moves from −0.125 under the baseline to +0.028 through +0.210 months/year across six calibration settings. TK population-to-area frequency ratios move from 1.005 to 0.931–0.979. XJ moves from 0.904 to 1.025 with the offset ending in 2013. CA stays above 1 under these calibration windows but reaches 0.844 under an assumed −3 °C shift. Ratios, rankings and lists must therefore be interpreted with their parameter settings.

The table separates model spread from joint time-resampling ranges for population counts. The latter use 500 draws of 25 full calendar years. Each draw re-estimates thresholds and offsets together, computes final indicators by model, then takes the main 14-model median. The future ten-year fields remain fixed.

Moving the calibration endpoint later does not reduce error monotonically. A single endpoint experiment cannot establish future error bounds. NASA Terra orbit information supports examining observation-time changes alongside clear-sky sampling, regional change and interannual variability. Aqua, matched locations and seasons, and overpass-time matching can help distinguish these candidate mechanisms.

Models share some structures, so their members cannot be treated as independent observations in significance tests of future differences. Record calibration windows, population sources and member influence separately. Whole-year resampling does not cover multiyear dependence or future nonstationarity; ±1 °C and ±3 °C shifts are stress settings. 7. Member influence and single-model use

Maximum changes are 0.118 months/year for 14→15 count medians and a 0.261 span for leave-one-model-out ensembles, across four regions, two windows, two scenarios and five weights. They do not establish unchanged sites. Retain member distributions; shared model structures preclude treating members as independent observations for future significance.

8. Data checks and next validation

Independent recomputation from raw daily tables finds the largest change in mean daily valid-point fraction between 2023–2024 and 2000–2019 in AU: 0.03832664, or 3.832664 percentage points. Similar fractions do not prove identical locations or weather were sampled. Next checks require matched locations, seasons and observation times. Spatial follow-up is in the project synthesis.

9. Screening purpose

Sequence data verification using contributions and stability before deciding on detailed asset or agricultural assessments. Population, buildings and cropland have distinct data needs.

12. Candidate locations and verification sequence

Population, built surface and cropland receive separate candidate lists. Within each model, cells are scored by exposure times exceedance frequency, then the median score across models is taken. The quota is one tenth of positive-exposure cells, rounded up. Selection proceeds from the highest score, retaining only positive scores and all ties at the boundary after rounding scores to eight decimal places.

Priority candidates must also be retained under all six calibration settings, the 15-model ensemble and every ensemble formed by leaving out one main-ensemble model. Both model selection share and joint time-resampling selection frequency must be at least 80%. Population candidates must additionally survive the switch to GPW. The top-decile and 80% thresholds are proposed scheduling rules for this project.

List stability means continued selection under the tested settings; selection frequency is not a disaster probability. Population lists organize resident-data checks, built-surface lists guide asset-use and occupancy checks, and cropland lists guide crop and season data collection. Loss or adaptation assessments require those inputs and independent observations.

13. Layer-specific implementation and testing

Add settlements, activity and seasonal occupancy for population; asset footprints, uses and occupancy for built surface; crops, growing seasons and irrigation for cropland. The priority rule sequences limited survey resources. Resolve source-sensitive and boundary cells, then validate hazard frequency with independent observations. Loss functions and adaptation costs require subsequent real-world data.
