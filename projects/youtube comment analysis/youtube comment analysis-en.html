Pet-ownership signals are extracted from 5.819 million channel comments, weak-supervision models are compared, and comment/user scores are translated into a relevance × candidate-scale framework for channel pilots.

Executive summary

This project analyzes 5,818,979 comments from pet-focused YouTube channels to identify textual evidence of pet ownership and shortlist channels for further evaluation. Verified owner labels are unavailable, so interpretable rules create weak-supervision labels. The models use TF-IDF plus comment, user and creator features; logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost provide the comparison, while SHAP is used to inspect model behavior.

Restricted-vocabulary XGBoost reaches 0.1915 rule-label test AP, above logistic regression at 0.1179 and random forest at 0.1110. RF uses a smaller sample, so learner choice alone cannot explain the differences.

A separate assistant review of 300 new comments uses 294 decidable labels as the reference, yielding conditional weighted precision of 0.8982 and recall of 0.4045.

Behavior analysis finds that 67.1% of users comment once and 85.1% appear under one creator. Channel selection should combine flagged-user share with candidate count, followed by comment review and small pilots; model scores indicate textual evidence, while verified identity, purchase intent and advertising return require other data.

1 The business question: pet-ownership text signals and channel shortlisting

Channel selection motivates this analysis. The corpus contains comments, not purchases, browsing exposure or verified household ownership. Non-owners may also buy pet products; no purchasing distinction is measured here.

The analysis identifies pet-ownership signals in comment text to prioritize channel review: among each channel's observed commenters, what share has a maximum within-channel comment score at or above the threshold, and how can that share and the flagged count guide review?

The main methodological challenge is the lack of verified owner labels for training and evaluation. The project therefore separates the task into two steps: interpretable rules create weak-supervision labels for comparing models, then a new set of comments that was not used for modeling receives model-assisted review for recognizable ownership evidence. The output supports channel ranking rather than identity verification or conversion prediction. The denominator includes only observed commenters, and flags represent textual signals; this share does not estimate verified pet ownership across the channel's full audience.

2 The corpus: composition and preprocessing of a scrape

The course dataset contains comments from pet-focused YouTube channels in three columns (creator_name, userid, comment). Dropping 491 empty comments from 5,819,470 raw rows leaves 5,818,979 rows, 2,536,890 users and 3,858 creators. No user ID is null; 32,040 rows have a null creator name. Comments average 77.3 characters, with a median of 43 and a 95th percentile of 249.

The following are descriptive properties of the collected corpus. The collection design may affect them, but its contribution is not identified:

  • 67.1% of users comment exactly once.
  • 85.1% of users appear under a single creator.
  • The top 38 creators hold 66.1% of all comments.
  • The Gini of comments across creators is 0.944.

This is a creator-comment dump, not a user panel. A panel would record what a user watched; this records what a user happened to say under channels the scrape covered. Any user-level aggregate below therefore describes commenting, which is what the scrape captured.

A 200,000-comment script sample gives 93.90% Latin, 2.64% Cyrillic, 1.86% Hangul, 0.14% CJK/Japanese, 0.10% Arabic, 0.08% other and 1.28% without letters. Latin script is not an English-language classifier. TF-IDF uses ASCII letter tokens and English stop words; training and full-corpus scoring do not filter to an English or Latin subset. Language-specific validity is unverified.

3 Labels: three labeling functions and the cost of weak supervision

The goal

Produce a training label for 5.8M comments when no owner label exists.

The choice

Rules train the classifier. Section 6 separates the historical 300-comment conditional P80 analysis from the new 300-comment evaluation at a frozen rule threshold. It does not measure a labeling budget or establish that alternative annotation is infeasible. Three explicit labeling functions make the proxy target inspectable:

Function Sign Keys on Examples
LF_strong_possessive +1 first-person possession "my dog", "we adopted a kitten", "I'm a dog mom"
LF_medium_context +1 weaker ownership context "my vet", "his crate", "when I feed my"
LF_negation −1 high-precision non-ownership "I want a dog", "my friend's cat", "don't have a pet"

The rule

A comment is labeled positive when a positive function fires and negation does not. Nothing is learned at this stage; the label is a regex over text.

The result

The positive rate is 1.52% (88,420 comments), an imbalance of about 1:65. Coverage is carried almost entirely by the strong rule at 1.466% (85,299 comments); the medium rule fires on 0.301% (17,521) and negation on 0.070% (4,049).

The standalone care detector is not directly used to construct y_rule, but its words overlap the labeling functions: vet, feed, walk, train, adopt, groom, leash and litter are not independent of rule construction. Retaining care_term_count therefore does not establish leakage-free generalization.

The cost

Two costs, and both propagate to the end of the report.

A rule-negative row can be a non-owner, an owner without a matched phrase, or a negation match. It is not confirmed non-ownership; nor are all negatives known positives. The implemented learner is ordinary rule-label classification, not a fitted positive-unlabeled correction model. Rule recall does not estimate true ownership recall.

The second is that the label inherits the rules' blind spots. An owner who never writes a possession phrase is labeled negative, and the model is trained to agree. Section 6 samples exactly that region on purpose.

4 Features: decoupling from the rule, and the ablation design

The model drops explicit rule columns and label-rate aggregates while retaining text length, care-term counts and user/creator behavior. The restricted vocabulary removes 39 specified words; the full vocabulary provides a configuration comparison. Care terms still overlap some rule words, so the vocabulary difference does not isolate the contribution of rule reconstruction.

Behavior features remain aggregated over the full corpus, including evaluation covariates. Evaluation is therefore transductive within this corpus and does not certify new-user, future-data or actual ownership performance. TF-IDF and IDF fit within each inner training fold to keep evaluation rows out of vocabulary learning.

5 Modeling: baselines, model choice, and tuning

User IDs define fit, selection and test roles, excluding all 299 users in the historical rubric sample. The roles contain 775,567, 260,296 and 344,882 comments. Three-fold grouped search retains the ten candidate configurations. Each fold fits its own vectorizer and class weight; rule-label F1 selects parameters.

The restricted XGBoost uses 450 trees, depth 8, learning rate 0.15, subsample 0.9, colsample_bytree 0.9 and min_child_weight 1. LR and RF provide references. RF uses a smaller sample, so differences cannot be attributed solely to the learner.

All metrics target rule labels. At roughly 1.5% positive prevalence, AP accompanies ROC-AUC; model ranking is not verified identity ranking. Prior test inspection makes these retrospective results.

6 Rule evaluation, historical labels and new assistant review

Frozen threshold and rule evaluation

τ_F1=0.848171115 maximizes rule-label F1 only on the 260,296 selection comments before evaluation on 344,882 test comments. Rule precision is 0.2386, recall 0.3101 and F1 0.2697. Of 6,609 flags, 1,577 have positive rule labels. These values do not measure pet-owner identity accuracy.

New 300-comment model-assisted evaluation

Eligibility requires users absent from model fitting, threshold selection, rule-label testing and historical review roles, nonempty comments with stripped length of at least five characters, and Latin letters comprising at least 0.9 of alphabetic characters. Five exclusive strata use priority negation, strong match, medium match, care and no signal. The S1-S5 sample counts are 60, 60, 30, 60 and 90. Each stratum uses simple random sampling without replacement, with inclusion probability n_h/N_h and comments as sampling units.

Stratum Sample size
S1 strong match without negation 60
S2 medium-only match without negation 60
S3 negation 30
S4 care only 60
S5 no signal 90

The assistant assigned textual labels without displayed scores or strata; scoring followed annotation sealing. Subsequent row-by-row review occurred after scoring and was not independent blind annotation. No labels changed: 85 support current or former household pet ownership, 209 lack supporting evidence and six are ambiguous. Actual human validation did not occur.

Precision is not the unweighted ratio 34/54. Metrics are weighted ratio point estimates conditional on decidable textual labels within the declared eligible population. There are no confidence intervals, full-corpus inference or precision-at-least-80% guarantees.

Conditional evaluation of the historical 300 comments

Frozen-model scores give conditional precision 0.8692 and recall 0.2369 on that sample. The user-grouped P80 selection procedure gives pooled precision 0.3881, recall 0.2152 and F1 0.2769. Fold thresholds and infeasible states remain separate; there is no validated single P80 deployment threshold. This sample is not merged with the new 300 comments, and historical intervals are not reused.

7 Results: what the model learned, and how far it generalizes

The frozen benchmark and full-corpus run store their model, vocabulary, threshold and sample scores separately. The full-corpus run regenerated both model and scoring outputs; its restricted-model scores match the frozen benchmark exactly on the checked test, selection and historical review rows. This consistency supports the calculations used in the report but does not extend label evidence beyond the new sample.

The full-corpus model scored 5,818,979 comments. Taking each user's maximum comment score flags 94,817 of 2,536,890 users, or 3.74%, at the frozen F1 threshold. More comments create more opportunities to cross the threshold. Comment precision cannot be converted directly into a count of actual owners. There is no single P80 threshold and therefore no P80 corpus user count.

Model-configuration differences and error analysis

Under the current rule-label test protocol, restricted XGBoost exceeds LR ROC-AUC by 0.0446 and raises AP from 0.1179 to 0.1915, a relative increase of about 62%. This supports retaining the restricted XGBoost configuration in this protocol, but the gap does not identify how it combines length, care context, user activity and creator structure. Full-vocabulary XGBoost reaches AP 0.3309, above the restricted configuration; the comparison does not isolate contributions from vocabulary, rule reconstruction or other settings. The SHAP results below describe fitted-model behavior without providing a mechanism ablation.

In SHAP, word_count and comment_len have the widest effects: longer, more complete narratives are generally more likely to receive high scores. Higher care_term_count also pushes scores upward. The model uses care context and narrative detail alongside animal names.

The failure modes are visible as well. Tokens such as tf::que, tf::por and tf::los expose language or register shortcuts; creator-structure features such as c_rank_log and c_is_top38 show that channel popularity participates in ranking. Performance within this corpus does not imply stable performance across languages, channels or time. Candidate ranking with review is a better use than automatic identity classification.

8 Full-corpus scoring and candidate rankings

Creator ranking requires at least 1,000 observed commenters and uses each user's maximum comment score within that creator to calculate flag share. The denominator is observed commenters, not the channel's full audience, reach or buyers.

The first five candidates show that share and scale are different selection criteria:

Channel Users above threshold Observed users Flagged share
Zak Georges Dog Training rEvolution 6,574 13,404 49.0%
Lennon The Bunny 2,881 6,488 44.4%
StormyRabbits 1,821 4,285 42.5%
Cole & Marmalade 8,755 31,883 27.5%
HammyLux 2,114 8,459 25.0%

Zak George combines a high share with substantial scale and is a sensible high-relevance pilot. Cole & Marmalade pairs a 27.5% flagged share with 8,755 users above threshold, the largest count in the table; it becomes more attractive when the objective is candidate reach. Lennon The Bunny and StormyRabbits offer high shares at smaller scale and fit lower-budget, higher-relevance tests.

This supports a concrete channel-selection framework: segment channels on flagged share × flagged-user count into high-relevance/high-scale, high-relevance/small-scale and moderate-relevance/high-scale groups, then review comments within each group. Shares and counts set candidate priority; they are not validated advertising outcomes.

9 Channel screening and further evaluation

Actions available now

  1. Use flagged share as channel relevance and flagged-user count as candidate scale; shortlist channels that score well on both or match the campaign objective.
  2. Rank comments within each shortlisted channel, review the top k, and record the share that contains clear pet-ownership evidence, called precision@k. Sample middle-score and near-threshold comments separately to inspect boundary cases.
  3. Split high-scoring comments into care questions, emotional engagement and general discussion so educational content, brand engagement and product research can use different pilot messages.
  4. Begin with a small-budget test or offline list review. Compare review yield, candidate scale and content themes before moving to a live advertising experiment.

Next model iteration

  • Compare user-level max, mean, top-k average and comment-count-adjusted aggregation, then measure channel-rank stability.
  • Evaluate by language or character-script stratum and monitor shortcuts carried by tokens such as que, por and los.
  • Refit without creator-popularity features and compare AP, the top-k review pass rate and channel rankings to determine whether the model learns comment content or channel identity.
  • If the objective changes from candidate ranking to probability or automated action, add independent human labels, calibration, confidence intervals and time-based validation.

Evaluation scope

Evaluation uses pet-ownership language captured by rule labels and assistant-reviewed labels as its reference. The new sample covers the filtered eligible comment population, with 294 decidable labels and a conditional weighted precision point estimate of 0.8982.

Behavior features use full-corpus aggregates, and user-level max favors users with more comments. Further evaluation can account for language, comment counts and channel-rank stability to improve candidate discovery and manual review.
