Executive summary

The analysis identifies risk segmentation by loan term, credit grade and revolving utilization, and compares three classifiers with simple approval rules. Random Forest captures more defaults, but false flags weaken its cost advantage. The term rule delivers a similar gross simulated cost result with less complexity. The gap between gross and net reductions also shows that policy choice depends on how errors are priced.

All analytical fields for 9,004 completed loans come from one full-data CSV, with originations from August to December 2011. The protocol uses 5,402 fit rows for training and cost calibration, 1,351 selection rows for model and threshold choice, and 2,251 test rows for frozen-policy evaluation. Random Forest minimizes selection gross cost subject to recall of at least 75%, at threshold 0.22.

Test ROC-AUC is 0.7252 and AP is 0.3535; recall is 70.45%, below the selection constraint. Gross simulated cost is 23.76% below approve-all, with a conditional 95% bootstrap interval of [16.33%, 30.49%]. Net reduction is 4.68%, without a corresponding interval. The term rule reduces gross cost by 24.01%, so the model does not improve that point estimate. This retrospective evaluation uses an already inspected test set and does not certify future gains or performance on rejected applicants.

The proposed next step is to retain simple rules as comparison baselines, pilot model scores for review prioritization, and build a checklist around term, grade and repayment burden. Thresholds should reflect processing capacity and actual costs. Recording risk coverage, false flags and review outcomes would help determine whether added model complexity is worthwhile.

1 The price of an error

The loan sample contains 9,004 rows and 30 columns, with all analytical fields drawn from the same full-data snapshot. There are 319 missing cells: 316 in employment length and three in utilization. About 16.8% of loans charged off.

Fit outcomes alone determine unit costs. Mean unrecovered principal among charged-off loans gives gross FN cost of $9,343.77; mean interest among fully paid loans gives FP opportunity cost of $2,267.79. Wrong-decline loss is a scenario assumption with no observed rejected-loan counterfactual. Crediting interest received from charged-off loans gives net FN cost of $6,632.16. Actual review, funding and operating costs are absent.

2 The concentration of default

In this sample, default rates rise from 7.4% at grade A to 52.9% at grade G; 60-month loans have 32.4% default versus 11.3% for 36-month loans. Verified borrowers show 19.4% versus 14.4% for unverified borrowers. These are group associations. Selective verification of riskier applicants is a possible explanation, not a tested mechanism; the analysis does not estimate effects of changing term or verification status.

Across roughly 9,000 rows, interest rate has Cohen's d 0.73 and utilization 0.31; the other listed effects are smaller. Open accounts have p=0.59 and d=-0.02, providing no significant group-mean difference in this test. This does not establish absence of univariate or model-predictive information.

Default rises across the saved utilization bins, from 10.5% at no more than 20% utilization to 23.7% above 80%, without a middle-bin dip. This does not rule out different relationships under other bins or controls, or establish a protective or safe band. Income groups show 20.5% below $40K and 11.1% above $100K. DTI reaches 20.2% in the 20-25 bin before falling to 17.0% in the top bin, with the extract capped near 30. Section 9's model attribution does not provide a causal test of these associations.

Grades E through G account for 9.4% of sampled loans and 22.0% of defaults. Among states with at least 50 loans, Nevada has 27.4% default against the sample's 16.8%. These concentrations do not control borrower composition or directly establish state- or grade-based underwriting rules.

3 Feature processing and information boundaries

Credit history is the difference between origination month and earliest-credit month divided by 12, without clipping. All 9,004 date pairs are valid and ordered; median history is 12.75 years. Both raw dates are then excluded from prediction. Eleven removed fields also include ID, funded amounts and repayment outcomes; feature engineering leaves 22 predictors.

The 316 missing employment-length values are first encoded as zero, together with employment below one year, without a separate missingness flag. This combines unreported and short employment histories. Missing utilization and derived numeric values are handled inside the pipeline. Capping, median imputation, category encoding, SMOTE and LR scaling fit inside the relevant training fold. State and purpose use TargetEncoder, and home ownership uses one-hot encoding. Encoded inputs do not all retain original units. Role isolation limits preprocessing access to evaluation rows, while actual underwriting-time availability remains independently unverified.

4 Three models under one protocol

The seed-42 stratified 75/25 outer split is retained, with 20% of outer training reserved for selection. Fit, selection and test contain 5,402, 1,351 and 2,251 loans. Three-fold GridSearchCV selects parameters by AP on fit rows only: eight LR configurations, 12 Random Forest configurations and 16 LightGBM configurations. AP denotes average precision.

LR selects C=0.05 and L1. Random Forest selects 200 trees, unrestricted depth and minimum leaf size 20. LightGBM selects 200 trees, learning rate 0.03, 15 leaves and unrestricted depth. Models finish fitting on fit rows without adding selection rows.

The fixed-parameter SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) comparison uses the same selection rows and AP (average precision).

5 The threshold under asymmetric cost

Thresholds are scanned only on selection rows from 0.05 through 0.94 in steps of 0.01. Gross cost is minimized subject to recall of at least 0.75; a model with no feasible threshold stops policy evaluation. Random Forest selection recall is 76.75% at 0.22. Model, costs and threshold are frozen before test evaluation.

Decline 60-month terms 24.74% 48.02% 24.01%

The model catches more defaults and flags more loans than the term rule. The opportunity cost of additional false flags offsets part of the saving from avoided default losses, leaving the model with a slightly higher gross cost point estimate. Higher recall and lower cost therefore need not move together. Selection-set choice does not guarantee test superiority over simple rules. No model-minus-term-rule interval is available, so the point difference does not establish significant superiority or inferiority.

7 The price of the recall floor

Random Forest flags 996 of 2,251 test loans, or 44.25%: 267 charged-off loans and 729 fully paid loans. It misses 112 defaults. The selection recall constraint is a policy-choice condition, not a test or production guarantee.

These counts have no measured review queue, processing capacity, recovery rate or operating cost. Use in an actual review workflow needs separate business validation.

8 The accounting behind the saving

Gross FN cost is $9,343.77, net FN cost is $6,632.16 and FP cost is $2,267.79. Selection under net accounting still chooses 0.22. Test net cost is 4.68% below the corresponding net approve-all baseline. No net-reduction interval is available; the gross interval cannot be reused.

Crediting interest already received lowers the unit loss of a missed default while leaving the opportunity cost of flagging a fully paid loan unchanged. Capturing defaults therefore yields less simulated saving, and false flags offset more of that saving. This accounts for the lower net reduction and explains sensitivity to the cost definition.

Nine cost-ratio scenarios from 3:1 through 5:1 each freeze their threshold on selection rows and all choose 0.22. This describes the checked grid only, without extrapolation to arbitrary prices or operating conditions.

9 Feature attribution for model predictions

SHAP shows how features contribute to the selected Random Forest predictions, using 2,251 test loans and 24 transformed features. Mean absolute SHAP ranks term, grade and the grade-term interaction first, followed by income and interest rate. The model therefore relies substantially on term and credit-risk segmentation to distinguish predicted risk.

The plots use processed feature values. Capping limits extreme numeric values to bounds learned within training folds, imputation fills missing values, and encoding converts categories into numeric inputs. Readers should distinguish original amounts and ratios from encoded values rather than treating every axis value as an original business unit.

The direction of the interest-rate association with model predictions agrees with the higher interest rates observed for charged-off loans in exploratory analysis. This helps explain which risk signals the model uses, but does not imply that lowering interest rates would reduce default.

Correlations between feature values and their SHAP values are +0.94 for grade, -0.76 for income, +0.92 for interest rate and +0.78 for the financial-stress index. They establish neither global monotonicity nor effects of changing loan terms.

10 Risk policy and implementation proposals

The analysis identifies risk segmentation by loan term, credit grade and revolving utilization, and compares three classifiers with simple approval rules. Random Forest captures more defaults, but false flags weaken its cost advantage. The term rule delivers a similar gross simulated cost result with less complexity. The gap between gross and net reductions also shows that policy choice depends on how errors are priced.

The proposed next step is to retain simple rules as comparison baselines, pilot model scores for review prioritization, and build a checklist around term, grade and repayment burden. Thresholds should reflect processing capacity and actual costs. Recording risk coverage, false flags and review outcomes would help determine whether added model complexity is worthwhile.

Retain simple rules as comparison baselines

The term rule flags 24.74% of test loans, recalls 48.02% of defaults and reduces gross cost by 24.01%; the model figures are 44.25%, 70.45% and 23.76%. Future comparisons should retain both the term rule and model, recording coverage, flag share and cost on the same data and accounting basis. The rule provides a benchmark for added complexity, rather than a ready-made automatic decline policy.

Pilot review ranking within processing capacity

Of the 996 test loans flagged by the model, 729 were ultimately fully paid. Turning every flag into a decline would therefore create substantial false-decline burden. A proposed pilot would first set the number of cases staff can process per period, then rank model scores and check documents in batches, retaining human decisions and recording handling time, findings and eventual outcomes. Ranking scores are not established calibrated default probabilities, and the offline decline simulation does not measure human-review gains.

Turn risk patterns into a review checklist

Segmentation by term, grade and utilization, together with model use of term, grade and financial-stress information, provides leads for document review. The proposed checklist would cover term, grade, revolving utilization, consistency of income, debt and installments, and room to meet repayments. It would organize checks without treating any feature as a cause of default or claiming that the combined screening conditions have been validated.

Choose thresholds using actual error costs and capacity

First specify false-decline opportunity cost, missed-default loss and per-case review expense, then compare candidate thresholds for coverage and workload within agreed capacity. The historical threshold of 0.22 is a comparison starting point, not an established deployment threshold. A new threshold should be evaluated on independent subsequent data, recording whether improvement over the rule baseline covers the added processing cost.

Scope of the conclusions

This evaluation uses a historical completed-loan cohort, random splits and offline simulations under specified costs. Further work can test ranking stability in new time windows and align feature timing, employment-history encoding and cost assumptions with the approval workflow.
